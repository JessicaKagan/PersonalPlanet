This is a brainstorming ticket. Personal Planet is very simulation-heavy, and it's going to need a couple of layers ready in order for us to implement meaningful gameplay. For a game about civilizations and societies in particular, creating a world full of life to interact with (sustainably or exploitably) is an important step.

The simulation flow most likely looks something like this:
1. The **World** is seeded with plants and animals (this is heavily simplified given the diversity of life).
    * What sort of life you can expect in a **Tile** depends on its conditions. Oceans will be full of fish and algae, desert life will be well adapted for high heat and limited water, tundras will be sparsely populated, etc. Lifeforms are most likely to thrive if the climate's favorable, but will sometimes appear outside its usual biomes.
    * Life will also affect the climate and overall conditions of a **Tile** over time! Case in point - if an area is full of big herbivores, they'll eat low-albedo plants and potentially contribute to the tile cooling over time. If something kills them off, though (like carnivores), the increased plant growth could increase the effective insolation and end up warming the tile! ...if we simulate an atmosphere, mind you, more plants will also sequester carbon and potentially cool the world simply by reducing the strength of the greenhouse effect.
2. Life will move through the world over time, most likely in a pseudo-random fashion. Plant seeds blown around by the wind, animals migrating, etc.
3. Over time, you should generally expect to see gradual changes in the carrying capacity of a **Tile**, but major changes in your world could result in these happening very suddenly! These potentially get more volatile as we add more layers, and especially once we start adding in civilizations that can affect the world more quickly and more intentionally.

In order to support a life layer, we need at least the following changes:
* First, we create a **Lifeform** interface. We should be able to define **Lifeforms** very quickly (and admittedly abstractly) from a list of properties. 
    * At a minimum, name, preferred climate attributes, size, and dietary preferences (if they can eat). Dwarf Fortress might be a good source of inspiration for metadata.
* Add a `life` property to the **Tile** class. Tiles will contain large amounts of **Lifeforms**, and most of them won't be notable enough that we need to simulate their behavior individually. Therefore, the `life` property should contain an array of _types_ of **Lifeforms** in the tile.    
    * We'll definitely need to track the number of lifeforms of each type, but I'm not clear yet on what else is worth simulating here.
    * If you're familiar with the pop systems in Paradox's games, you'll have a sense of what needs to be done - streamlined modeling of group behavior.
* Add functions for computing the behavior of the **Lifeforms** in each tile. These get called periodically in `Game.updateSimulation()`.
    * Tile life needs to be updated more frequently than tile climate, but less frequently than tile civilization/society info in the long run.
    * During each relevant simulation update, life can potentially spread to new tiles, attempt to feed on other life, and reproduce. How successfully they are is going to depend on not only the climate and life in their current tile, but (at least for moving around the world) the state of other tiles.
    * A lot of these interactions need to happen synchronously! I want to avoid situations where the life simulation's results per tick vary depending on which tile was updated first, or what order we update individual lifeforms, etc. There's a lot of unknown unknowns here about how to handle concurrency issues, avoiding race conditions, etc.
    * Running a **Lifeform** update tick, therefore, is probably going to be split into two steps. In the first, each tile's **Lifeforms** "choose" their actions. In the second, we evaluate if there's any conflicts between the actions they want to take (most likely reproduction after a round of predation? Keep thinking about this) and attempt to resolve them, whether that's by weighted-averaging together expected results, having some actions preempt others, etc. The "civilization" layer will also need something along these lines at some point, so figuring out how to deal with things at this point is a good chance to determine best principles for game actor conflict resolution at some point.
